# Tally adds up published results but never presents a result or a winner

Tally sums the polling-unit results on INEC's portal for each area and shows that sum beside the announced result. It always labels the figure as a sum of portal data with its coverage, shows it only as a comparison, and never names a winner or ranks candidates.

Section 124(4) of the Electoral Act 2026 makes it an offence, with four years' imprisonment, for "any person" to announce or publish "an election result knowing same to be false or which is at variance with the signed certificate of return". The second limb has no knowledge requirement and has not been tested against a third-party tally, so a figure presented as a rival result is the main exposure. The Act creates no offence for adding up published figures, and INEC said on 17 November 2026 that independent groups are free to "collect, analyse and independently verify" as long as private figures are not "communicated as officially declared results" and the portal is named as the source.

This is a reading of the text, not legal advice. Other laws, such as the Cybercrimes Act, were not reviewed.

## Considered options

- **Show differences only, never a sum.** Safest, but the difference is meaningless without the two figures it comes from.
- **Publish Tally's own totals and standings.** Rejected: it reads as a competing declaration, which is what INEC objects to and what Kenya's media houses had to withdraw in 2022.

## Consequences

- Coverage is always shown next to a portal sum, because a partial sum compared with a full announced figure misleads on its own.
- Tally flags an unexplained mismatch only when the missing polling units could not account for the gap.
